Antony takes the East, meets **Cleopatra at Tarsus (41) and becomes her lover** — three children (twins Alexander Helios and Cleopatra Selene, Ptolemy Philadelphus). Next year Octavian enters Alexandria (Aug 30) → **Antony's suicide** (on a false report of Cleopatra's death); **Cleopatra, failing to win Octavian over, kills herself rather than be paraded in his triumph** (aged 39; the asp tradition). **Caesarion (17) is executed** — "two Caesars is one too many". - West: Latin → Romance languages; East: Greek — a split that persists today.
- Cleopatra was a Macedonian Greek; her ties to Caesar and to Antony were both political choices to keep Egypt from becoming a Roman province. Both times she ended up on the losing side of a Roman civil war.

# Western Europe (Middle Ages → Modern) {#europe}

The western half, cut off at 476 in the [Rome table](#rome). Roman inheritance (Latin, law, the Church) fuses with the Germanic kingdoms into medieval Europe, then passes through Renaissance, Reformation and revolution into the modern age.

- The rhythm of European history: fall of Rome → medieval Church + Germanic kingdoms → Renaissance and Reformation dissolve Church authority → absolutism → revolutions make nation-states → industrialization and imperialism → world wars.
- The Holy Roman Empire, as Voltaire said, was "neither holy, nor Roman, nor an empire", yet it lasted 844 years (962~1806) until Napoleon dissolved it.

# Iran (Persia · Parthia · Sasanians → modern Iran) {#iran}

**Persia ≠ Parthia.** Both Iranian, but different peoples and dynasties. The line of rule over Iran: **Media → Achaemenid Persia → Seleucids (Greek) → Parthia → Sasanian Persia → Islam → … → Safavids → Qajars → Pahlavis → Islamic Republic.** The Greeks fought the Achaemenids; Rome fought Parthia and the Sasanians.

- Old Persian (Achaemenid) was written in cuneiform, Middle Persian (Parthian, Sasanian) in Aramaic-derived scripts, and New Persian (Farsi) after Islam in the Arabic script.
- "Persia" is what the Greeks called the whole country after the region of Pārsa (today's Fars). The native name since the Sasanians is "Iran" (Ērān, "land of the Aryans").

But after taking Constantinople in 1453 they also styled themselves "Caesar of Rome" (Kayser-i Rûm), which ties them to the last line of the Eastern Roman table. # The Mongol Empire {#mongol}

The protagonist of the 13th century, scattered across the other tables until now. It runs through [Korea](#korea) (invasions of Goryeo), [China](#china) (Yuan), [Iran](#iran) (Ilkhanate), [Islam](#islam) (sack of Baghdad), [Western Europe](#europe) (Legnica), [Japan](#japan) (the Mongol invasions) and [India](#india) (Mughals).

| Mughal Empire | 1526~1857 | **Babur, Akbar, Shah Jahan, Aurangzeb** | Babur (Timur's descendant) wins at Panipat (1526). **Akbar** (1556~1605): religious tolerance, administrative reform. Shah Jahan's **Taj Mahal** (1653). Aurangzeb's hard-line Islamic policy → revolts and decline. **Plassey (1757)**: the British East India Company takes Bengal |
| British India | 1858~1947 | Queen Victoria, **Gandhi**, Nehru, Jinnah | Sepoy Rebellion (1857) → Company abolished, direct Crown rule. Victoria Empress of India (1877). Indian National Congress (1885). Gandhi's non-violent civil disobedience (Salt March 1930) |
| Independence · Partition | 1947~ | Nehru, Jinnah | **Independence (15 Aug 1947)** — India (Hindu) and Pakistan (Muslim) separated amid bloody mass migration. Bangladesh independent (1971). Republican constitution (1950) |

- Indian history is not a single chain of dynasties like China's: unified northern empires (Maurya, Gupta, Mughal) rise intermittently, with regional kingdoms in between.
- Buddhism virtually vanished from India, its birthplace, by the 12th century (absorbed by Hinduism, then the Islamic invasions) and survived in Southeast and East Asia.

# The Americas (Maya · Aztec · Inca) {#americas}

Developed in complete isolation from Eurasia. In common: no iron, no practical wheel, no large domestic animals, no horses. Only the Maya had a full writing system. After European contact (1492~) they collapsed rapidly under smallpox and conquest.

## Mesoamerica (Mexico · Central America) {#mesoamerica}

| Civilization | Years | Centres / figures | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Olmec | c. BC 1200~400 | San Lorenzo, La Venta | **Mother culture of Mesoamerica.** Colossal heads, jaguar cult; prototypes of the ballgame, calendar and writing |
| Maya | c. BC 2000~AD 1697 (Classic 250~900) | Tikal, Palenque, Copán, Chichén Itzá. King Pakal the Great (615~683) | **Full logo-syllabic script**, **the concept of zero**, the Long Count calendar (epoch BC 3114). City-states, never a unified empire. Classic collapse (9th c.; drought and warfare) → shift to northern Yucatán. Last city, Tayasal, falls in 1697 |
| Teotihuacan | BC 100~AD 550 | Pyramids of the Sun and Moon | City of 100,000+. Builders unknown. Later revered by the Aztecs ("city of the gods") |
| Toltec | c. 900~1150 | Tula | Revered by the Aztecs as cultural ancestors. The Quetzalcoatl myth |
| **Aztec (Mexica)** | **1325~1521** | **Tenochtitlan** (1325, on Lake Texcoco). Itzcoatl — Triple Alliance (1428). **Moctezuma II** (1502~1520) | Empire built on the **Triple Alliance** of Tenochtitlan, Texcoco and Tlacopan. Human sacrifice, *chinampa* floating fields, pictographic writing. **Cortés** lands (1519) → allies with subject peoples such as Tlaxcala; smallpox → **fall of Tenochtitlan (13 Aug 1521)**; last emperor Cuauhtémoc. Mexico City built on the ruins |

## Andes (Peru · Bolivia) {#andes}

| Civilization | Years | Centres / figures | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Caral (Norte Chico) | c. BC 3000~1800 | Caral | **Oldest urban civilization in the Americas**, contemporary with the Egyptian pyramids |
| Chavín | c. BC 900~200 | Chavín de Huántar | First pan-Andean religious culture |

| **Inca (Tawantinsuyu)** | **1438~1533** (Kingdom of Cusco from c. 1200) | **Cusco**. Legendary founder Manco Cápac. **Pachacuti** (1438~1471) builds the empire; Machu Picchu. Túpac Yupanqui; Huayna Cápac (1493~1527) at greatest extent | "The land of the four quarters". 4,000 km from Colombia to Chile. **No writing** — records kept on knotted-cord ***quipu***. 40,000 km of roads, *chasqui* runners, terraced farming, *mit'a* labour. Smallpox kills Huayna Cápac → **civil war** (Huáscar vs Atahualpa). **Pizarro** seizes **Atahualpa at Cajamarca (16 Nov 1532)**, executes him (1533). - **Why so few Spaniards prevailed:** Old World diseases such as smallpox (up to 90% population loss), horses, steel and firearms, defection of subject peoples (Aztecs), division right after a civil war (Incas), and the tactic of seizing the ruler.
- (ref.) North America: the Mississippian culture — Cahokia (800~1350, great mounds near St. Louis); the Pueblo (Ancestral Puebloan) culture of the Southwest.
